The best activities for high-energy kids give a clear target for all that motion: timed races and hill rolls and chase games outside, then floor-level obstacle courses, crab-walk relays, and animal-move challenges when you're stuck indoors. The trick isn't burning a wound-up kid out until they collapse, it's channeling the energy into a mission with a start, a finish, and a reason to go hard. A kid told to "settle down" fights you. A kid told "beat your own time around the yard" runs three laps and comes in grinning.

Because a high-energy kid isn't a problem to fix. That fizzing, can't-sit-still, bouncing-off-the-walls energy is just fuel with nowhere to go, and your job isn't to switch it off. **It's to point it at something.** Give the motion a shape, a goal, and a finish line, and the same wound-up kid who was climbing the furniture two minutes ago is fully, happily absorbed.

## Big-movement outdoor missions

Outside is where high-energy kids belong, and the games that work best are the ones that never really stop moving:

- **Turn running into a chase.** Plain laps get boring fast, but a chase never does. [Shadow Tag](/app/quest/shadow-tag) flips regular tag on its head: instead of tagging a body, you stomp on someone's shadow, so your kid is sprinting, dodging, and reading the sun's angle all at once.
- **Let them roll.** Sometimes the fastest way to drain a full tank is to get low and let gravity help. [The Hill Roll](/app/quest/the-hill-roll) is exactly what it sounds like, a grassy slope and full-body rolls to the bottom, and the climb back up is half the workout.
- **Race the water.** On a hot day, nothing burns energy like a soaked, shrieking backyard competition. [The Big Splash Olympics](/app/quest/the-big-splash-olympics) strings together sponge relays, water-balloon events, and splash challenges into a whole afternoon of go, go, go.
- **Set a timer and beat it.** High-energy kids love a number to chase. Time one lap of the yard, then dare them to shave a second off.

## Build a course they run over and over

Obstacle courses are the secret weapon for a high-energy kid, because they combine two things a wound-up brain craves: hard physical bursts and a puzzle to solve.

## When you're stuck indoors

Rain, dark, a small apartment, a sick day. Sometimes going outside just isn't on the table, and a high-energy kid stuck inside can feel like a pinball. The fix is to bring the big movements indoors, in a version the floor and the neighbors can survive:

- **Animal moves down the hallway.** Bear crawl one way, crab walk back, frog jumps for the last stretch. It's real exertion, low to the ground, and quiet on the feet.
- **Keep the balloon alive.** One balloon, one rule: it can't touch the floor. It's the rare indoor game that's genuinely high-effort but gentle on downstairs neighbors.
- **Cushion-course the living room.** Couch cushions and a taped floor line become a lava course to cross without touching the ground. It's an obstacle course that fits between the coffee table and the couch.
- **Run a "how many" challenge.** How many star jumps in a minute? Give a wound-up kid a number to chase and they'll pour everything into hitting it.

Keep indoor games light on the feet (crawling, balancing, tossing soft things) and save the real stomping and jumping for the yard, where it belongs.
