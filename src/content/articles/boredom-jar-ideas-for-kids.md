A boredom jar is a jar filled with slips of paper, one activity per slip, that your kid draws from whenever they say "I'm bored." To make one, grab any jar (a mason jar, an old peanut butter tub, a coffee can) and either cut paper into slips or use flat craft sticks you write on with a marker. The trick that makes the whole thing work is what you write: a slip that says **"build a fort out of every cushion in the house"** gets done, while a slip that says "play" gets groaned at and stuffed back in. Sort the slips by indoor and outdoor, or by how long they take, so the draw fits the moment. Then make it a ritual: "I'm bored" means go pull a slip, no negotiating, no picking through for the easy one.

Because the real magic of a boredom jar isn't the jar. **It's that the decision is already made.** A bored kid doesn't actually want a list of options; a list is just more work. They want a single thing handed to them, and they want it to sound like an adventure instead of a chore. The jar does the deciding so nobody has to argue about it, and randomness turns picking a slip into a tiny thrill, like a scratch-off ticket where the prize is a plan.

## How to build the jar (and write slips that actually work)

The physical part takes ten minutes. The slips are what you'll fine-tune over weeks.

- **Pick your slip style.** Paper slips are free and fast, but they get soggy and torn; **craft sticks** (the wide popsicle kind) survive being drawn a hundred times and are easier for little hands to grab. Write one activity per slip in marker.
- **Be specific, always.** "Go outside" is a slip your kid will refuse. "Find a stick shaped like a sword and name it" is a mission they'll run to do. [The Stick Sword Hunt](/app/quest/the-stick-sword-hunt) is exactly this kind of slip: a clear goal, a bit of story, a reason to move.
- **Make each slip a complete thought.** It should say what to do and, ideally, what "done" looks like. "Stack pebbles" is vague. "Stack pebbles into the tallest tower you can before it topples" has a finish line.
- **Aim for zero-setup.** The best slips need only what's already in your house or yard. If a slip requires you to dig out glitter and a hot glue gun, it'll never get drawn on a Tuesday.

## Sort the slips so the draw fits the moment

One undifferentiated jar means your kid might pull "go to the park" during a downpour. A little sorting fixes that.

- **Split by indoor and outdoor.** Two jars, or one jar with two colors of slip (blue for inside, green for outside). Rainy day? Draw from the indoor pile.
- **Or sort by time.** Mark each slip with a rough clock: a **5-minute** quickie for the gap before dinner, a **30-minute** builder for a long afternoon. Now the jar answers "I'm bored and we leave in ten minutes" and "I'm bored and it's a whole Saturday" with equal ease.
- **Keep a "big adventure" corner.** A few slips that take real time and pay off big, like [The Secret Fort](/app/quest/the-secret-fort), which turns a corner of the living room into a hideout with a proper build to it. These are the ones your kid will start hoping to draw.

## Slip ideas: outdoor missions

Fill the outdoor side with things that have a goal, not just "run around." A goal is what a bored kid is missing.

- **Go on [The Stick Sword Hunt](/app/quest/the-stick-sword-hunt)** and find the perfect stick, then test its balance and give it a name.
- **Become a [Pebble Tower Champion](/app/quest/pebble-tower-champion)** by stacking the tallest, wobbliest stone tower you can before gravity wins.
- **Draw [The Treasure Map](/app/quest/the-treasure-map)** of your own yard or block, with an X marking a hidden "treasure" you bury or stash.
- **Count ten living things** you can find outside, from ants to birds to the moss on a wall.
- **Find five things older than you are** out there. (Rocks, a full-grown tree, the sidewalk cracks. It's a surprisingly good puzzle.)

## Slip ideas: indoor and quiet

A scavenger hunt makes an excellent slip because one line of writing buys twenty minutes: "find five round things" is a whole game. There are forty more like it in our [indoor scavenger hunt ideas](/articles/indoor-scavenger-hunt-for-kids/).

For the rainy days, the wound-up evenings, and the ten minutes before you can get out the door.

- **Play [Cloud Zoo](/app/quest/cloud-zoo)** from the window and spot as many animals hiding in the clouds as you can before one drifts apart.
- **Build the tallest tower** you can out of anything soft: cushions, blocks, laundry, books.
- **Set a 10-minute treasure hunt** where you hide a small toy and draw clues for whoever finds it next.
- **Invent a new game** with one ball and one rule, then teach it to someone in the house.
- **Make up a story** about the last object you touched and tell it out loud like it's the most important news of the day.
