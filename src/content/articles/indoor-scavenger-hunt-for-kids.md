The fastest indoor scavenger hunt needs zero printing and zero prep: pick a rule and send them off. Call out a color and have them bring back five things that match it, or a texture (something soft, something bumpy, something cold), or a sound (something that jingles, something that crinkles). For readers, hunt by first letter ("find something that starts with B") or by shape ("bring me three round things"). Each round takes two minutes to invent and ten minutes to play, and every one of them uses stuff you already own. No worksheet, no store run, no setup.

Here's why this beats the printable hunts you find online: **a printable locks you into one list, but a rule scales to any kid and any room.** The same "find something soft" works for a toddler and a nine-year-old; you just change what counts and how fast. So instead of hunting for the perfect PDF, learn a handful of hunt *types* you can pull out anywhere, from the living room to a hotel room to grandma's kitchen.

## The ready-made indoor scavenger hunt list

If you want a list right now, take this one. Read the items out loud, or write the ones you like on a scrap of paper and hand it over. Everything here is in an ordinary house, nothing needs buying, and no item is breakable or sharp.

**In the living room**

- Something softer than your pillow
- Something with a button on it
- Something older than you are
- Something that makes a sound when you shake it
- Something with a number printed on it
- Something you could hide behind
- Something that does not belong in this room
- Something with more than four corners
- Something you have never noticed before
- Something that would float

**In the kitchen**

- Something cold
- Something that smells strong
- Something that came from a plant
- Something round enough to roll
- Something with a handle
- Something see-through
- Something that fits inside a cup
- Something with a lid
- Something exactly as long as your hand
- Something that would be heavy if you filled it

**In a bedroom or bathroom**

- Something that used to be your favourite
- Something with your initial on it
- Something you wear only in winter
- Something in a colour nobody else in the house owns
- Something that smells like you
- Something you could balance on your head
- Something with a zip
- Something that was a present
- Something small enough to lose
- Something you would take on a desert island

**Anywhere in the house (the hard ones)**

- Something bumpy AND blue
- Something that starts with the same letter as your name
- Two things that match but are different sizes
- Something that has been in the house longer than you have
- Something that would fit in a shoebox and something that would not
- Something worth exactly nothing and something worth a lot
- Something with writing so small you have to squint
- Something you can see through but cannot walk through
- Something that would still work if the power went out
- Something you are allowed to keep

That is forty items, which is far more than one hunt needs. Pick eight or ten, or let your kid pick. An indoor treasure hunt lives or dies on how fast it starts, so a short list you begin immediately beats a long one you are still writing when the kid wanders off.

Then, when you have used the list a few times and want hunts you never have to write down at all, keep reading.

## Hunt by color, shape, and letter

These are the fastest to start because the rule fits in one sentence:

- **Color hunt.** Name a color and set a number: "Find five red things and line them up on the couch." Kids scan a familiar room in a completely new way when they're filtering for one color. [The Color Match](/app/quest/the-color-match) turns this into a proper mission with steps to follow and gems at the end, so it feels like a game and not a chore.
- **Go big on one color.** Narrow it further for a fun twist: hunt only for yellow, and only for *big* yellow things. [The Big Yellow Hunt](/app/quest/the-big-yellow-hunt) does exactly that, and the "big only" rule makes kids think about size and color at the same time.
- **Shape hunt.** "Bring me three round things, two square things, one triangle." Great for younger kids who are still learning shapes, and it doubles as a quiet math warm-up.
- **First-letter hunt.** For readers: "Find something that starts with the letter T." Bump it to "find one thing for each letter of your name" and you've got a ten-minute hunt out of a single prompt.

## Hunt by texture, smell, and sound

Color and shape are visual. These next hunts use the other senses, which makes them feel fresh and works beautifully for kids who aren't reading yet:

- **Texture hunt.** Call out feelings instead of things: "Bring me something soft, something scratchy, something smooth, something bumpy." [The Soft and Scratchy Hunt](/app/quest/the-soft-and-scratchy-hunt) is built around this, and it gets kids really handling the world instead of just looking at it.
- **Smell hunt.** Send them sniffing: "Find something that smells nice, something that smells strong, something with no smell at all." [The Smell Hunt](/app/quest/the-smell-hunt) is a small delight, and the kitchen and bathroom become the most interesting rooms in the house.
- **Sound hunt.** "Find three things that make noise, and rank them loudest to quietest." A set of keys, a crinkly wrapper, a spoon on a mug. This one gets loud, so save it for a high-energy afternoon.
- **Temperature hunt.** "Bring me something cold and something warm." Quick, surprising, and perfect for toddlers who love a simple sorting job.

## Match the hunt to the age

The same hunt bends up or down with tiny tweaks:

- **Toddlers (2 to 4).** Keep it to one rule and one item at a time: "Can you find something blue?" Cheer hard when they bring it back. No lists, no counting, just fetch-and-celebrate.
- **Little kids (5 to 7).** Add a number and a category: "Find five soft things." They can hold a small goal in their head and love reporting the total back to you.
- **Bigger kids (8 and up).** Stack rules to make it a puzzle: "Find something that's soft AND blue AND smaller than your hand." Combining conditions is where it gets genuinely challenging.
- **Mixed ages.** Give each kid a different rule at the same time so nobody's hunt is too easy or too hard, then have them all report back together.

## Make it harder or turn it into a race

Once the basic hunt is easy, crank the difficulty without any new materials:

- **Add a timer.** "Everything on the list in three minutes, go." A countdown turns a calm hunt into a sprint and burns real energy.
- **Stack the rules.** One condition is a warm-up; three at once is a brain-bender. "Round, red, and makes a sound" can take a while to satisfy.
- **Team race.** Split into two teams (or kid vs. parent), same list, first to finish wins. Siblings who bicker over everything will suddenly cooperate to beat you.
- **The put-it-back rule.** Best trick in the book: the hunt isn't done until every found item is returned to where it came from. Your kid gets a game, you get a tidy room, everybody wins.
